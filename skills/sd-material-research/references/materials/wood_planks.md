# Sawn wood planks (decking, floorboards, cladding): reference sheet

> **Scope:** sawn boards in rows: decking and boardwalks, interior floorboards (strip, plank, tongue-and-groove),
> cladding (bevel/lap, board-and-batten, drop or barn siding). Softwoods (pines, Douglas-fir, western redcedar,
> redwood) and hardwoods (red and white oak, ipe). Unfinished, penetrating finishes (oil, water repellent,
> semitransparent stain) and film finishes (paint, solid stain, varnish); new to heavily weathered. **Not covered:**
> plywood and OSB (unprotected face veneer can be destroyed within 10 years [4]), laminate and engineered look-alikes,
> logs, end-grain blocks, wood-plastic composites, shingles and shakes. Species-specific detail (e.g. cedar
> extractive staining) is left to live research.
> **Researched:** 2026-10-02, revised 2026-10-03. **Confidence:** erosion, relief, shrinkage, hardness, layout codes,
> knot limits and gloss readings are measured or code values (USDA FPL, IRC, NWFA, NLGA, papers). Cup magnitudes are
> lab values on offcuts. North American species colours, roughness (derived from gloss by a stated model), and check,
> halo and wear sizes are estimates, marked (est.). physicallybased.info has no wood entries [37].

Every number carries a source tag `[n]` (see Sources) or `(est.)` with a one-line reason.
Give ranges rather than single values, and say which variant or region a value applies to.

## 0. Physical summary
A sawn plank is a slice through a log's growth-ring structure: nested conical shells of low-density earlywood and
dense latewood (latewood can be more than 3x as dense as earlywood in the same southern pine ring [19]), crossed by
radial rays and by branches (knots) that run out from the pith. The saw fixes the figure on each face, and
installation fixes each board's footprint, the gaps or overlaps between boards, and the fastener rows over joists or
studs. Aging then acts on each board separately. Four facts are most often wrong in procedural versions.
(1) **Weathering removes wood from the board and reveals more of the same board.** Earlywood erodes faster than
latewood, so latewood ridges, knots and fastener heads end up standing proud because the wood around them went down.
Relief is only about 0.1-0.35 mm after 6 years on a deck (from the 6-year rates in [3]) and 0.55-0.9 mm after 16
years on a south-facing wall [1]. (Young boards also show raised grain without any loss: crushed latewood recovers
when planed wood takes up water [40].)
(2) **The weathered skin has two layers.** A thin grey, leached, fungus-coloured fibre layer sits on a yellow-brown
photodegraded zone; fresh-coloured wood starts only 0.5-2.5 mm down [12][13][15]. Light abrasion shows brown, not
fresh wood. Fresh wood differs from grey in hue and saturation, not always in lightness: pale pine is lighter than
grey, while cedar, redwood and ipe can be darker.
(3) **Wear and erosion round the arrises and hollow the faces of the boards. They never widen the gap.** Gap width
is set by the layout and by the board's moisture content. The gap shows what is beside and below the board (joist
tops, darkness, debris), never "more gap".
(4) **Grey is not a uniform desaturation.** It forms only where light, rain (which leaches the photodegraded lignin)
and dark fungi act together [12][14]. Faces under eaves, behind battens and inside lap overlaps stay brown or keep
their original colour [4][14]; in humid climates sheltered faces go dark and blotchy from mildew instead [4].
Interior floors darken and amber; they never go grey (est., no rain leaching and no fungal colonisation [12][14]).

## 1. Construction and layout

### 1.1 The board itself (what the saw fixes)
- **Ring angle.** Rings at 0-45 deg to the wide face = plainsawn (flat-grain, slash-grain). Rings at 45-90 deg =
  quartersawn (edge- or vertical-grain). Hardwood with rings at 30-60 deg is sometimes called bastard-sawn [5].
  Oak flooring is sold as plainsawn, quartersawn (50% quartered character) or rift (75% rift character) [7].
- **Flat-sawn figure.** Each conical growth shell meets a flat face along a U or V curve, so plainsawn faces show
  nested "cathedral" arches in the middle of the face. Near the edges the face is far from tangential to the rings [5],
  so the lines become straighter and tighter (rift-like runout). Geometry (derived): with ring width w and ring-to-face
  angle theta, the band width seen on the face is w / sin(theta). At the arch apex theta is near 0 and the bands
  widen into "flames". At theta = 90 deg (quartersawn) the bands are their true width. Because the log tapers,
  the arch apexes point toward the top of the tree, spaced along the board at roughly w / (radial taper per unit
  length), e.g. 3 mm / 0.005 = 0.6 m (est.: the International 1/4-inch log rule assumes 1/2 in of diameter per
  4 ft, about 5 mm of radius per m; taper-sawn outer boards see less).
- **Quartersawn figure.** Parallel straight stripes. Oak adds ray "fleck" (silver grain) [7]. Oak rays are several
  cm high [5]: more than 32 mm in the white-oak group and less than 25 mm in red oak [33]. On plainsawn oak, rays show
  as short dark ticks along the grain (est.).
- **Earlywood and latewood.** The transition is abrupt in southern yellow pine, Douglas-fir, larch and redwood, and
  gradual in western redcedar, spruce and true firs [5]. Southern pine specific gravity is 0.21-0.35 for earlywood
  and 0.56-0.72 for latewood [19]. "Dense" southern pine has at least 6 rings per inch (ring 4.2 mm or less) and at
  least 1/3 latewood, or at least 4 rings per inch with at least 1/2 latewood [20]. Western redcedar and redwood have
  thin latewood bands; southern pine and Douglas-fir have wide ones [1][4].
- **Sapwood and heartwood.** Sapwood is the light band next to the bark [5], one pale edge stripe or a whole pale
  board. Widths: WRC up to 2.5 cm (nearly white); redwood narrow (almost white); DF narrow in old growth, up to 7 cm
  in second growth; southern pine 2-5 cm, wide in second growth; oak 2-5 cm; ipe wide, yellowish-grey and sharply
  defined [10]. Sapwood carries no extractives, so it never bleeds tannin [4].
- **Knots.** On flat-sawn faces knots are round or oval; on quartersawn faces they are cut lengthwise as spike knots
  [5]. Intergrown ("red", live) knots are continuous with the stem wood and disturb the grain more. Encased ("black",
  dead) knots are not continuous with it [5]; they can loosen in drying [8] and fall out as knot holes [21].
  Pine knots cluster in whorls along the length (est., at the annual height growth, about 0.3-1 m).
- **Slope of grain.** Fibres not parallel to the long edge run out of the edge diagonally. The FPL example board has a
  slope of 1 in 7 [5]. Spiral and interlocked grain cannot be sawn straight [5]. Ipe is often narrowly interlocked
  [10], which gives ribbon stripes on quartersawn faces (est.).
- **Machined surface.** Planed (S4S) faces carry knife marks: 13-15 per inch from an ordinary planer and 21-22 per
  inch for AWI millwork (1.2-2.0 mm pitch) [34]. Rough-sawn faces carry saw marks across the board: straight from a
  band saw, arcs from a circular saw (est., a few mm pitch). Grades allow light "skips" (unplaned spots) [21].
  Radius-edge decking (RED) has edge radii of 6.4 mm at 25 mm thick and 9.5 mm at 38 mm thick [7]. Dimension lumber
  has eased edges of about 3 mm (est.).

### 1.2 Decking and boardwalks
- Boards: 5/4 RED at 25 x 140 mm or 2x6 at 38 x 140 mm, 2.4-4.9 m long [7]. Do not exceed 6-inch nominal
  (140 mm actual) to limit cupping [24]. For best resistance to cupping, width should be at most 8x thickness [4].
- Joists at 12, 16 or 24 in on centre. Maximum spacing: 1-1/4 in decking 16 in (406 mm) multispan, 12 in single
  span; 2 in decking 24 in (610 mm); diagonal decking 12 and 24 in [22]. Installers limit RED to 16 in [24].
- Each board bears on at least 3 joists [23]. **Two nails or two screws at every joist** [22][23] (one for boards
  2 in wide, two for 4 in and wider [29]). Fasteners sit about 19-25 mm in from each edge (est., common practice to
  avoid edge splits). Pre-drill any fastener within 6 in (152 mm) of a board end [24]. Hidden clips show none.
- Gaps: wet lumber goes down tight and dries to about 1/8 in (3 mm) [23]; dry boards are spaced about 1/8 in [24].
  UC ANR recommends at least 1/4 in (6 mm), noting that debris still collects in square-edged gaps; bevelled
  (undercut) edges let it fall through [32].
- Butt joints land on doubled joists so each end has full bearing, and they are staggered row to row [24][32].
  Decking overhangs the rim by about 25-50 mm (est.; no code limit was found [22][23]).
- Ring orientation: SFPA tells installers to lay boards bark-side up [24]; FPL says to expose flat-grain siding
  bark-side out [4]; others say lay the best face up, because in service the moisture gradient decides the cup [39].
  Trials found bark-side-up deck boards cupped and checked more, while pith-side-up boards shelled [17][18]. Real
  decks mix both (est.).

### 1.3 Interior floorboards
- Strip: 19 mm (3/4 in) thick; face widths 38, 51, 57 and 83 mm; random lengths from 0.23 to 2.6 m. Average lengths
  by oak grade: Clear 1.14 m, Select 0.99 m, No. 1 Common 0.84 m, No. 2 Common 0.69 m [7]. Plank is 3 in (76 mm)
  and wider [25].
- Tongue-and-groove, end-matched [7]. Blind-nailed through the tongue every 8-10 in (solid strip) or 6-8 in (solid
  plank), and 1-3 in from every end joint [25]. **No fastener shows on the face** except face nails on the start and
  finish rows: every 10-12 in on strip, 8-10 in on plank [25]. Old wide-plank floors are often face-nailed in rows on
  the joists (est.).
- End joints: stagger row to row by at least twice the board width (6 in for 3 in boards, 10 in for 5 in plank)
  [25]; the 2007 guide gave at least 6 in (152 mm) for strip [26]. Use max(152 mm, 2 x width). Distribute lengths
  randomly from several bundles. Avoid H-joints, visible stair-steps and equal offsets in successive rows [25].
- Seasonal movement: a 2-1/4 in board changing 1/16 in (1.6 mm) adds up to an inch over 16 boards [26]. NWFA uses a
  red oak DCC of 0.00369 per % MC per inch [25]; FPL gives 0.00304 tangential and 0.00137 radial for northern red
  oak (6-14% MC) [8]: use the FPL values for plainsawn and quartersawn boards respectively. Vertical-grain flooring
  wears more evenly and its joints open less [7]. A dry-season joint opens onto the unfinished tongue (est.).

### 1.4 Cladding
- **Bevel (lap) siding.** Overlap 1 in for 4-6 in nominal, 1 to 1-1/8 in for 8 in, 1 to 1-1/2 in for 10 in and 1-2 in
  for 12 in. One nail per piece per stud, at most 24 in on centre, placed just above the overlap so it never pierces
  two courses. Butt joints are staggered and fall on studs [27]. Exposure = actual width - overlap. A 6 in nominal
  board (140 mm actual, est.) at 1 in overlap gives about 115 mm.
- **Board-and-batten.** Real Cedar: 6 in boards spaced 1/2 in (13 mm) apart with battens lapping each board by at
  least 1/2 in, more for wider boards; 1x3 battens (64 mm) over 1x10 boards (235 mm) is a suggested combination
  (1 in lap per side at a 1/2 in gap). Boards up to 6 in take one centre nail per bearing; boards 8 in and wider take
  two nails 2-1/2 to 3 in apart. Each batten nail passes through the gap between the boards [28].
- **Drop, rustic or barn siding.** Often flat-grain and often knotty grades [4][7]. Barn boards are usually
  rough-sawn and 150-300 mm wide, with gaps from drying shrinkage (est.). Rainscreen furring is 9-19 mm [4].

### 1.5 What the layout fixes
These footprints are set at construction, and no aging process may change them: board edge lines at the as-built top
plane, gap widths (at a given moisture state), end-joint positions, joist and stud lines and the fastener positions
on them, siding course and butt lines, overlap zones, batten footprints and tongue-and-groove lines. Inside each board
the saw also fixes the ring field, the sapwood/heartwood zones and the knot positions. The installation and
maintenance history fixes a **per-board finish state** (bare, penetrating finish fresh or worn, film) and board age
(replaced boards are newer): these are layout-level attributes, because boards are replaced and refinished as units
or in areas, not by a noise (est.). Moisture state (gap width, cup, raised grain, knot shrinkage) is a separate
per-variant **state** parameter, applied the same way to the `nowear` comparison render.

Beneath and beside, per product:
- **Deck:** below a board is more board (25-38 mm), then the joist. Beside it is the gap, and through the gap you see
  the joist top (38 mm wide strips at the joist spacing), darkness, ground, or debris filling the gap.
- **Floor:** below is the subfloor; beside is a tight joint. A dry-season joint opens onto the unfinished tongue
  (est.). On worn or repeatedly sanded floors, once the wear layer above the tongue (about 6 mm on 19 mm strip, est.)
  is gone, the groove lip breaks away and the neighbour's tongue and the blind nails show: a named exception.
- **Lap siding:** below each course's butt edge is the top of the course beneath. That overlap zone is hidden and
  unweathered. Behind it are the sheathing and furring.
- **Board-and-batten:** the gap between boards is covered by a batten. Under the batten the board faces are
  unweathered: original height and original colour.

**Original surface envelope.** The as-built top surface of each board: its machined profile (edge radius, eased
edges, knife or saw marks, profiled grooves), plus that variant's moisture-state shape (cup or crown, raised grain,
knot shrinkage relief), which the `nowear` render also carries. Removal processes (erosion, wear, power washing,
sanding, checks, decay, chipping) can only go below it. Deposits (dirt, moss, mildew, film finish, water) sit above
it. A penetrating finish adds no height. Three things legitimately rise and need their own masks: lifted latewood
slivers from shelling [18], fastener heads backing out of the wood [4], and fibre "fuzz" (tens of um, est.).

## 4. Processes: aging, wear, damage, deposition

### 4.1 Photodegradation and colour change (fresh, then yellow-brown, then grey)
- **Mechanism:** UV and violet light degrade lignin in the top 50-300 um [13]. Photodegraded lignin yellows and
  browns the wood (S2). Rain leaches it from the very surface, leaving cellulose-rich fibres (S1), and
  black-pigmented fungi (Aureobasidium pullulans) colonise them, which reads as grey [12][13][14].
- **Acts on:** S1 and S2. **Removes / adds:** removes lignin and extractive colour; adds thin fungal pigment.
  **Reveals:** under S1 the yellow-brown S2 of the same board; fresh wood only below the colour-change depth
  (0.5-2.5 mm [15]). Wasps stripping the grey fibres leave warmer stripes [12]. Deep splits and chips show fresh
  colour until they re-weather. A cleaned, leached surface looks coloured when wet and silver when dry [38].
- **Never:** greys faces that rain does not wash (inferred: grey needs the leaching that [14] describes); they stay
  yellow-brown in dry climates and go blotchy dark grey from mildew in humid ones [4]. Never greys wood under intact
  paint, under battens or inside overlaps (a covered plywood strip stayed intact after 10 years [4]). Never greys
  interior floors (est., no leaching and no fungi [12][14]). Changes colour only; height loss is section 4.2.
- **Where:** sun- and rain-exposed faces. Exposure is harshest on south and west walls (northern hemisphere), where
  paint also fails first [4][30], and on horizontal or 45-degree faces [3]. A silvery grey forms where fungi are
  suppressed (hot arid climates, salty coastal air, some high mountains) [4][12]. In humid shade the grey is dark
  and blotchy [4].
- **Shape and scale:** edges between sheltered and exposed zones are sharp at hard edges (batten edge, overlap line)
  and graded under eaves, following the rain-wetting gradient (est.). Grey also covers latewood ridges and knots.
- **Progression:** stage 0 (minutes to weeks): light woods darken and yellow, dark woods fade [4][12]; Delta E*
  3-11 after 1 month [15]. Stage 1 (6-12 months): grey [12]; Delta E* 23-34 and L* down 16-31 after 12 months
  (pale European species) [15]. Stage 2 (several years): an even grey or silver patina, after which the appearance
  changes little [4]. Presets: new = stage 0 or none; weathered = 1-2; distressed = 2 plus biological darkening (4.5).
- **Interactions:** opens the surface to erosion (4.2) and checking (4.4); feeds mildew (4.5); paint adhesion falls
  after 2-3 weeks of exposure [4].
- **Signature per map:** albedo near neutral, L* about 40-62 depending on mildew (section 7), chroma C* below about
  6 (est. from [15]); roughness up (Ra 4-7 um rises to 8-25 um in 12 months [15]); no height change by itself.
- **Severity scale:** none for wood colour; use CIELAB Delta E* [15]. **Sources:** [4][12][13][14][15][30][38].

### 4.2 Surface erosion and differential relief (raised grain, knots and fasteners proud)
- **Mechanism:** loosened fibres wash away. The rate falls with density, so earlywood goes faster than latewood. Once
  latewood ridges are exposed they erode from the sides too, and thin bands break off, so the two rates converge
  (about 7 years in WRC and redwood; later in dense, wide-latewood DF and SP) [1][2].
- **Acts on:** S1, S2, then W. **Removes:** wood. **Reveals:** deeper wood of the same board and the same ring field.
  Latewood ridges, knots and fastener heads are left standing proud.
- **Never:** adds height; moves board edges or widens gaps; re-randomises the grain (the deeper slice cuts the same
  rings); erodes covered areas (under battens, overlaps, washers, intact paint) [4].
- **Where:** exposed faces. Erosion is about 2x faster at 45 deg than vertical; horizontal faces erode at about the
  45 deg rate or less, because degradation products and dirt shield them [3]. Arrises lose wood from two faces, so
  corners round (est., radius about equal to the eroded depth). Windborne sand, salt and ice abrade windward faces:
  on a pine box facing Antarctic blizzards, the soft layers were cut about 3 mm deep while the hard knotty fibres
  were barely touched [12].
- **Shape and scale:** vertical, south-facing, Wisconsin: 4.5 (SP), 6.0 (DF), 7.5 (redwood) and 9.5 (WRC) mm per
  century [1]; 4.0-6.0 for ponderosa, lodgepole, Engelmann spruce, hemlock and red alder [2]; about 6 on average
  [4]; WRC 12, DF 6, dense hardwoods 3 [12]. Horizontal, first 6 years, vertical-grain surfaces (earlywood/latewood,
  um/yr): DF 81/48, SP 102/44, WRC 135/77 [3] (red oak 114/105 is flagged by [3] as unreliable: large vessels and
  possible incipient decay). **Relief** (earlywood minus latewood), vertical: 0.17-0.30 mm at 4 years, 0.33-0.51 mm
  at 8 years and 0.55-0.91 mm at 16 years [1]. WRC levels off at 0.50-0.64 mm from 8 to 16 years and redwood reaches
  0.55 mm at 16; DF (0.91) and SP (0.83, partly decayed) are still rising at 16 years [1]. Horizontal softwoods: grows
  33-58 um/yr on vertical-grain and 13-47 um/yr on flat-grain faces [3]. Profile: valley-shaped earlywood and peaked
  latewood [1].
- **Early relief without removal (raised grain).** Planer knives crush hard latewood into the soft earlywood beneath;
  when the board later takes up water the crushed earlywood recovers and pushes the latewood up. It is worst on the
  pith side of flat-grain boards, grows over weeks after planing dry wood that then gets damp, and on edge grain the
  latewood stands out or sinks as the moisture content moves away from its value at planing [40]. FPL lists raised
  grain with checks as a main problem of unfinished decks [38]. No measured height was found: 0.05-0.3 mm (est.).
  Being a moisture-state shape of the planed surface, it belongs in the `nowear` render too.
- **Progression:** stage 1 (0-2 years): raised grain, fibres lifted, matte. Stage 2 (5-15 years): corrugation of
  0.2-0.6 mm, ridges catch grazing light. Stage 3 (decades): thin-latewood species (WRC, redwood) stay at about
  0.5-0.7 mm [1]; wide-latewood DF and SP keep diverging, 1-2 mm after many decades (est., extrapolated from [1]).
  Covered strips stand proud as weathering shadows, with a step equal to the total loss (4-10 mm per century,
  vertical [1][2]). Knots stand 0.5-2 mm proud after decades (est., limited by the total loss).
- **Interactions:** opens checks (4.4); loosens fasteners and leaves heads proud (4.7) [4]; traps dirt in the
  valleys (4.10). Decay speeds it up: the southern pine test boards decayed after 10-12 years [1].
- **Signature per map:** height = minus erosion weighted toward earlywood, reduced at knots and latewood; fine stripes
  in the normal along the grain; AO in the valleys; high roughness. Ridges catch highlights and valleys hold dirt
  (est.).
- **Severity scale:** none published; use erosion depth in um [1]. **Sources:** [1][2][3][4][12][38][40].

### 4.3 Moisture movement: shrinkage, gap change, cupping and crowning
- **Mechanism:** below fibre saturation (about 30% MC) wood shrinks and swells about 2x more tangentially than
  radially, and very little along the grain [6]. A moisture gradient through the board (sun-dried top, damp
  underside, finish on one side only) plus ring curvature makes boards cup or crown [4][6][39].
- **Acts on:** the whole board's shape. **Removes / adds:** nothing; it moves surfaces. **Reveals:** a wider gap,
  showing more of what is beside and below the board (joist top, debris, darkness); on T&G and shiplap, a strip of
  unweathered, unfinished tongue or lap (est.). Every point on a board edge moves together.
- **Never:** acts only on the skin; makes local holes; changes one board's width unevenly along its length (est.).
  It is a state of the layout, not wear.
- **Where:** decks, because sun keeps the top much drier than the air's RH suggests [4] while the underside sees damp
  air [39]. Floors over damp crawlspaces (est.). Siding with wet backs [4]. Flat-sawn boards cup and check more than
  quartersawn ones [4][5].
- **Direction:** in service on a deck the through-thickness gradient dominates and has the same sign for every
  board: the dry top shrinks, so boards cup edges-up in dry weather and flatten or crown after soaking rain [39].
  Ring curvature adds a per-board term: drying alone makes the bark-side face go concave (tangential about 2x radial
  [6]), so bark-side-up boards cup more and pith-side-up boards less [17][39]. Cup = edges above centre; crown =
  centre above edges [26].
- **Shape and scale:** width change = width x DCC x Delta MC. Tangential DCC per % MC: redwood 0.00205,
  WRC 0.00234, loblolly 0.00259, coast DF 0.00267, northern red oak 0.00304, white oak 0.00376 [8]. Ipe shrinks
  8.0% tangentially and 6.6% radially, green to ovendry [6]. Example: a 140 mm flat-sawn DF board drying from 19% to
  12% MC loses 2.6 mm of width (computed from [8]). Cup measured on 400 mm offcuts of ACQ-treated DF, hemlock and
  spruce (136 x 35 mm, flat-faced) drying in a conditioning room: 0.4 mm after 1 month and 1.4-1.6 mm after 6-8
  months; fastened boards weathered outdoors for 6 months cupped less; bark-side-up boards cupped more after
  weathering [17]. No cedar or pine was tested. Service cup: 0.2-1.5 mm (est., below the free lab values).
- **Knots.** Branch fibres in a face knot run through the thickness, where longitudinal shrinkage is tiny [6], while
  the board around them shrinks across its thickness; a dried board shows knots slightly proud and a wetted one
  slightly sunk, about 0.05-0.2 mm per face on 25 mm boards (est.: 25 mm x 0.0016 per % x 7% MC, split over two
  faces). This is a moisture-state shape, also in `nowear`.
- **Progression:** new: flat, design gaps. Weathered: seasonal gap swing, 0.2-1.5 mm cup (est.). Distressed:
  0.8-3 mm cup (est.), twisted ends, fasteners loosened [4].
- **Interactions:** cupped tops hold water, which feeds mildew, decay and dirt (4.5, 4.6, 4.10) [24]. The dry top
  shrinks and checks (4.4) [4]. Fast drying at ends causes end checks [8].
- **Signature per map:** height: a per-board low-frequency profile, edges up in the dry state, crowned in the wet
  state, magnitude varying 30-50% board to board (est., from ring orientation [17]). Normal: a broad gradient across
  each board. Dirt, AO and puddles collect at the low points (wet preset).
- **Severity scale:** grading rules limit cup qualitatively (very light, light) [21]. **Sources:**
  [4][5][6][8][17][21][24][26][39].

### 4.4 Weathering checks, end checks, splits and shelling
- **Mechanism:** the sun-dried surface shrinks past its elastic limit and fractures parallel to the grain [4].
  Photodegradation makes checking worse [17]. Checks start where cell walls change abruptly: growth-ring boundaries
  and ray/tracheid interfaces [12]. End checks come from fast drying at board ends [8]. Shelling is growth rings
  delaminating and lifting from pith-side-up faces [18].
- **Acts on:** face and ends, inside one board. **Removes:** nothing but crumbs; it opens a fissure. **Reveals:**
  shadow, then darker, less-weathered wood of the same board (est.), never the gap or the joist.
- **Never:** crosses board edges or carries on into the next board. Never runs across the grain on a face (cross-grain
  cracks come from paint or brown rot) [4][9]. Never appears on covered faces.
- **Where:** flat-grain faces more than vertical-grain [4][5]; centres of cathedral figure and boards cut near the
  pith (est.). Dense species check more than low-density ones [4]. Bark-side-up boards check more; pith-side-up
  boards shell [17][18]. End checks sit in the end zone. Splits start at fasteners near ends (which is why ends are
  pre-drilled) [24]. Encased knots check radially, loosen and drop out (knot holes, 4.12) [8][21].
- **Shape and scale:** 0.2-2 mm wide when dry and partly closed when wet, 20-300 mm long, up to 1/3 of the thickness
  deep (est.). End checks run radially on the end grain and 20-150 mm into the face (est.). On flat pith-side-up
  faces growth rings delaminate and lift, highest where rings meet the face at a low angle; profiled boards shed
  lanceolate latewood slivers (western larch) [18]; lift about 0.1-0.5 mm (est.).
- **Progression:** new: none, or only seasoning checks within grade limits (2 Common: 2 medium checks per 1x8x12 ft)
  [21]. Weathered: fine parallel checks on flat-grain faces. Distressed: wide checks, end splits, shelling.
- **Interactions:** water gets in, so decay follows (4.6). Dirt and mildew fill the checks. Paint cracks over them.
- **Signature per map:** height: narrow V-grooves along the grain; normal: sharp lines; AO and albedo: dark lines;
  roughness up. **Severity scale:** grading terms (small, medium, large checks; splits by length) [21].
  **Sources:** [4][5][8][9][12][17][18][21][24].

### 4.5 Mildew, mould, sapstain, algae, moss and lichen
- **Mechanism:** surface microfungi (mostly black, some red or green) grow on bare wood and on paint. Blue-stain
  fungi stain sapwood. Algae, mosses and lichens colonise weathered wood that stays damp [4][9][12].
- **Acts on:** the surface (deposit D) and sapwood (stain). **Adds:** pigment and biofilm; moss and lichen cushions.
  **Reveals:** nothing. Bleach and deck cleaners remove mildew [4][38]; sapstain goes into the sapwood and cannot be
  planed off [9].
- **Never:** erodes wood by itself [4]; grows evenly over sunny, rain-washed faces (est.); stains heartwood blue
  (stain fungi are mostly confined to sapwood [9]).
- **Where:** shaded, moist or protected areas: walls behind trees or shrubs where air is still, and dew-prone eaves
  and porch ceilings [4]. On decks: gaps with debris, board ends, cupped low points and shaded or north sides (est.).
  Drying oils (linseed, tung) feed mildew, especially on redwood and the cedars, so oiled wood darkens [4]. Water
  repellents without mildewcide stain blotchily [38]. Lichens grow on weathered decking [12].
- **Shape and scale:** mildew is blotchy dark-grey to black speckle; blue stain shows as radial wedges on end grain
  and as streaks or spots on faces [9]; moss cushions 5-30 mm across in gaps and along edges (est.).
- **Progression:** new: none. Weathered: mildew speckle in shade. Distressed: moss in gaps, green algae films, lichen
  rosettes on horizontal faces.
- **Interactions:** darkens the grey (4.1); grows with moisture, cupping and debris (4.3, 4.10); signals decay (4.6).
- **Signature per map:** albedo black speckle and green films; roughness high on moss; height moss up to a few mm
  (deposit mask). **Severity scale:** ASTM D3274, a non-linear pictorial scale of fungal, algal or dirt
  disfigurement [35]. **Sources:** [4][9][12][35][38].

### 4.6 Decay (rot)
- **Mechanism:** decay fungi need MC above fibre saturation (about 30%); air-dried wood at 20% or less does not decay
  [9]. Brown rot removes cellulose: the wood turns browner, cracks across the grain into cubes, shrinks and collapses.
  White rot removes lignin and cellulose: the wood bleaches, keeps its outline and feels spongy. Soft rot is shallow,
  on surfaces that are repeatedly wetted and dried [9].
- **Acts on:** W, starting at wet contact zones. **Removes:** wood (collapse, crumbs). **Reveals:** more decayed wood of
  the same board. Only in distressed variants does it break through to the joist (a named exception to 5.3).
- **Never:** starts in the middle of a sun-dried face (est.); occurs in wood kept at 20% MC or below [9].
- **Where:** board ends, butt joints, joist contact lines, fastener holes, gaps packed with debris, ground-contact ends
  and the bottom course of siding [9][32]. Contact surfaces hold water by capillarity and dry slowly [32].
- **Shape and scale:** brown cubes about 5-20 mm, sunk 1-5 mm (est.). It follows the wet zone: a band along a joist
  line or 50-300 mm in from an end (est.).
- **Progression:** distressed variants only. Incipient decay shows as mottled brown or bleached areas [9].
- **Signature per map:** height: depressions with a cubical crack network; albedo brown (brown rot) or bleached
  (white rot); roughness high. **Severity scale:** none read. **Sources:** [1][9][32].

### 4.7 Fasteners: rust, iron-tannin stain, extractive bleed, heads proud
- **Mechanism:** steel nails rust, giving a reddish-brown stain through or around the coating. Iron reacting with
  tannins gives a blue-black stain (oak, WRC, redwood). Water dissolves the extractives in WRC and redwood heartwood
  and leaves reddish-brown stains, including run-down streaks below lap joints when the backs get wet [4].
  Weathering loosens fasteners [4].
- **Acts on:** the skin around each fastener. **Adds:** colour, plus the rising head (fastener mask). **Reveals:** nothing.
- **Never:** appears without an iron source (steel fastener, steel wool, wire brushing) [4], or with stainless or
  aluminium fasteners (est.; recommended to avoid it [4][29]). Extractive bleed never comes from sapwood [4].
- **Where:** a halo round every steel head. On vertical cladding, streaks run downward from fasteners and lap joints
  [4]. Fastener rows follow the joists or studs (section 1).
- **Shape and scale:** halo radius 3-15 mm; streaks 20-300 mm long; blue-black at L* about 20-35 (all est.).
  Heads stand 0.5-5 mm proud from erosion around them plus withdrawal (est.; erosion alone gives 1.3-2.2 mm over
  20 years on a deck, from [3]).
- **Progression:** weathered: halos. Distressed: streaks, rusted heads, heads proud, some missing (empty holes).
  Extractive stains weather away within months once the water source stops [4]. **Signature per map:** albedo dark
  halo and streak; fastener height on its own mask; metallic only on bright heads.
- **Sources:** [3][4][29].

### 4.8 Foot-traffic wear and denting (decks, boardwalks, floors, stairs)
- **Mechanism:** grit under shoes abrades first the finish, then the wood. Earlywood wears faster than latewood.
  Point loads (furniture legs, heels) dent soft species [5][7][11] (rates est.).
- **Acts on:** F or P, then S1, S2 and W, and the arrises at steps and thresholds. **Removes:** finish and wood.
  **Reveals:** the same board's next layer: on a weathered deck, brown S2 in shallow lanes and fresh wood only where
  wear exceeds the colour-change depth; on painted floors, older coats or primer, then wood.
- **Never:** widens gaps or exposes the subfloor or joists (except the worn-T&G exception, section 3); spreads evenly
  over the whole tile; reaches under furniture or rugs (est.).
- **Where:** paths between doors, stairs and gates; thresholds; stair nosings; work spots such as in front of a sink
  (est.). Board edges across the traffic direction get rounded (est.).
- **Shape and scale:** dull lanes 0.3-1 m wide; finish worn through after years; old softwood floors hollowed 1-5 mm
  with latewood ridges of 0.3-2 mm and knots proud (est.). Flat-grain wears less evenly than vertical-grain [7];
  quartersawn faces raise less grain [5]. Side hardness at 12% MC: WRC 1,600 N, eastern white pine 1,700, old-growth
  redwood 2,100, loblolly 3,100, coast DF 3,200, longleaf 3,900, northern red oak 5,700, white oak 6,000, ipe 16,400
  [11]. So dents run 0.5-2 mm in WRC and pine and are rare in ipe (est.).
- **Progression:** new: none. Lived-in: dull lanes, scratches along the direction of travel. Worn: wear-through,
  hollows, rounded nosings 2-10 mm in radius (est.). **Signature per map:** roughness up in lanes (gloss to matte
  [31]); albedo warmer (S2) or dirtier; height: hollows, dents, rounded nosings; normal: fine scratches.
- **Sources:** [5][7][11][31].

### 4.9 Finishes: film vs penetrating, aging and failure
- **Two classes.** Film finishes (paint, solid-colour stain, varnish, floor polyurethane) are a deposit above the
  face with a measurable step at their edges. Penetrating finishes (oils, water repellents and water-repellent
  preservatives, oil-based semitransparent stains) absorb into the wood and form no film [4][38]: they live inside
  the top of S2 or W (layer P), so they have no edge step and do not peel. Waterborne coloured WRPs tend to form a
  thin film [38]. FPL recommends penetrating finishes for decks, because films trap moisture, wear in traffic and
  peel [38].
- **Penetrating finishes.** Darker and more saturated, like damp wood [16]; WRP gives a golden-tan on most species
  [4]. Life: WRP about 1 year on exposed deck surfaces [38]; nondrying (paraffin) oils hold no pigment and are
  short-lived, while pigment extends life to many years under light traffic [38]; semitransparent stain 2-3 years
  (new, smooth) or 3-6 (weathered, rough) on decks [4], up to 8 years as two wet coats on rough siding [30].
  Unpigmented finishes show no uneven wear or brush marks; pigmented stains show lap marks [38] and wear off first
  along traffic paths (est.). Drying oils feed mildew [4]. As the finish weathers out, the board resumes the grey
  trajectory of 4.1. Oil over an uncleaned grey surface saturates the grey and its mildew and reads dark grey-brown,
  not wood-coloured (est.); cleaning first restores the colour (4.13).
- **Paint and solid stain.** Paint fails first over the wide latewood bands of flat-grain boards [4], usually cracking
  parallel to the grain (across it when thick and many times repainted), chalking and cracking at joints; moisture
  is the usual cause, and south and west walls go first [4][30]. Two coats last 4-5 years, three up to 10 [30]. Solid
  stain flakes on decks [4][30]. Revealed: primer, then the same board's wood, fresh or grey by exposure time.
- **Clear exterior film (spar varnish, polyurethane):** at best about 2 years [30]; it peels within 6 months to
  2 years because light degrades the wood beneath it [12], showing patchy grey or dark wood (est.).
- **Interior clear floor finish:** 5 GU (ultra-matt) to 82 GU (gloss) [31]; wears in traffic lanes. Floor stains
  penetrate only slightly, so wear-through shows lighter, unstained wood [4].
- **Per-board finish state.** Decks mix replaced boards, boards refinished at different dates, and areas shaded by
  furniture, so the finish state is per board or per area (layout-level, section 1.5), not a noise (est.).
- **Never:** a film finish sits below the surface; a penetrating finish forms a step or peels; any finish loss
  reveals the gap; film peels under a stable roof overhang as fast as on an exposed wall (est.).
- **Shape and scale:** peeled film patches are long along the grain, with sharp edges and flakes curling 0.1-0.5 mm
  (est.). The paint film step is 0.09-0.13 mm [30].
- **Signature per map:** film: height step plus curled flakes on the deposit mask; albedo paint colour against
  exposed wood. Penetrating: albedo and roughness only, no height. Roughness: section 7.
- **Severity scale:** ISO 4628 pictorial standards: part 2 blistering, part 4 cracking, part 5 flaking [36].
  **Sources:** [4][12][16][30][31][36][38].

### 4.10 Dirt, debris and soil
- **Mechanism:** wind and feet carry debris into gaps and low points. It holds water and promotes mildew and decay [32].
- **Acts on:** gaps, cupped hollows, checks and earlywood valleys. **Adds:** a deposit. **Never:** builds up on
  rain-washed sloped faces (est.); sits on board tops more than a thin film (est.).
- **Where:** debris collects in square-edged gaps, including 6 mm ones [32]; along walls and railings; at butt joints
  and over joist lines [32]. **Scale:** fills the gap partly; leaves and needles 10-80 mm (est.).
- **Signature per map:** albedo dark brown-grey; high roughness; AO; height fills part of the gap depth (deposit mask).
- **Sources:** [32].

### 4.11 Wetting (transient state)
- **Mechanism:** water filling the pores and lumens changes the refractive contrast, so the surface darkens and its
  colour intensifies. Liquid water darkens far more than humid air does [16]. End grain absorbs water much faster than
  face grain [4].
- **Never:** lightens; changes geometry instantly (swelling takes hours to days, est.). **Where:** the whole exposed
  surface after rain; puddles in cupped boards [24]; end grain and gaps stay dark longest [4]; edges and ridges dry
  first (est.).
- **Scale:** on water submersion, temperate species: Delta L* -2.5 to -13.6, Delta a* +1.9 to +7.2, Delta b* +4.8 to
  +12.9, Delta E* 9-18 (28 under vacuum); wenge -14.4; darker woods darken more (R = 0.70) [16]. Strongly coloured
  tropical species did not gain chroma [16].
- **Signature per map:** albedo darker and more saturated; roughness 0.0-0.1 in puddles and 0.3-0.5 when damp (est.).
- **Sources:** [4][16][24].

### 4.12 Holes: knot holes and insects (optional detail)
Encased knots loosen and drop out, leaving holes the knot's size [8][21]. Carpenter bees bore perfectly round 13 mm
holes in unfinished softwood out of direct sun (porch-roof undersides, siding). Beetle exit holes are 0.8-7 mm, and
borer holes are 3-13 mm [9]. They are removal processes inside one board, on their own `hole` mask; a through hole
may reach gap level (a named exception to 5.3).

### 4.13 Maintenance: cleaning, power washing, sanding (decks)
- **Mechanism and reveals.** Deck cleaners and brighteners remove mildew and dirt (deposits) and reveal the S1 or S2
  colour; a fully leached surface stays silver when dry [38]. Aggressive caustic scrubbing or power washing removes
  wood, particularly soft western redcedar [38]: the soft earlywood furrows, fibres fuzz, and wand strokes leave
  bands (est.); it reveals S2 and, where deeper, fresh W. Sanding is a planar cut: it reaches latewood ridges and
  proud knots first, which turn fresh-coloured while earlywood valleys and checks stay grey until the cut passes the
  valley floor, about the relief plus the colour-change depth, 1-2 mm on old DF or SP (est. from [1][15]).
- **Never:** widens gaps or removes the gap's deposits by "erosion" (cleaning removes deposits, a separate mask);
  sanding never lowers the valleys before the ridges.
- **Where:** whole areas or per board (section 1.5); refinishing follows (4.9).
- **Signature per map:** albedo patches with sharp board-aligned edges; height: power-wash furrows along the grain
  (est. 0.1-0.5 mm), sanding flattens ridges; roughness: fuzz up, sanded down.
- **Sources:** [1][15][38].

## 5. Invariants
1. **Layout is fixed.** Gap, board ID, end-joint, joist, fastener, batten, overlap, exposure and finish-state masks
   come from the layout generator only. *Why:* boards are cut, fixed and maintained as units (section 1.5).
   *Enforce:* every downstream node reads these masks; no blur, warp or erosion output feeds back into them.
   *Check:* `mask_invariance` on gap, fastener, cover and finish_state against `nowear`, `max_changed_frac` 0.0005.
2. **Removal only goes down.** *Why:* erosion, wear, washing, sanding, checks and decay remove wood (4.2-4.13).
   *Enforce:* each removal is applied as min(h_nowear, h_nowear - removal); moisture-state shapes (cup, raised
   grain, knot shrinkage) are in `nowear` too; deposits (dirt, moss, film, fastener heads, shelling) come from
   separate masks; penetrating finish has no height. *Check:* `envelope` against `nowear`, except [deposit, film,
   fastener, shelling], `tolerance_mm` 0.05.
3. **Erosion reveals the same board, never the gap.** *Why:* below the face is 19-38 mm more of the same board
   (section 3). Named exceptions: knot and insect holes and decay break-through (distressed), and the worn-T&G lip
   (floors); each has its own mask. *Enforce:* removal depth is capped at a fraction of the thickness (est. 2 mm on
   decks, 10 mm on century-old siding) and clamped to H >= gap floor + 0.5 mm. *Check:* `order` upper = board_solid,
   lower = gap_floor (gap core minus deposits), `lower_stat` p95, `neighborhood_mm` 30, `margin_mm` 0.5,
   `max_violation_frac` 0.001, hard.
4. **Wear rounds arrises inside the footprint and does not widen gaps.** *Why:* the edge profile is part of the board
   [7]. *Enforce:* edge rounding is driven by a distance-to-edge field measured inward from the layout. *Check:*
   `run_length` on gap across boards, p50, equal to the `nowear` value within 1 px.
5. **Relief comes from earlywood going down, plus early raised grain.** *Why:* density-dependent erosion [1][3];
   machining recovery adds a small early relief without removal [40]. *Enforce:* latewood mask from the ring
   function; erosion = rate_EW x (1 - LW) + rate_LW x LW; raised grain as a moisture-state term in both renders.
   *Check:* `height_diff` a = latewood, b = earlywood, `local_mm` 8, p50, on the detail tile (section 9).
6. **Knots stand proud only through slower loss and dry-state shrinkage.** *Why:* erosion falls with density
   [1][12], knot wood is denser (est.), knotty fibres resisted abrasion [12], and knots barely shrink in the
   thickness direction [6]. *Enforce:* knot_factor reduces the removal; the shrinkage relief is a moisture-state term
   in `nowear`; nothing adds wood. *Check:* `height_diff` a = knot, b = knot_ring, `local_mm` 40, plus invariant 2.
7. **Grain runs with the board and is continuous through depth.** *Why:* the ring field belongs to the board's volume
   [5]. *Enforce:* rings are computed in board-local coordinates, evaluated at the eroded depth, with a new seed at
   every board edge or end joint. *Check:* `orientation` map = normal (basecolor on new boards), region = face,
   `axis_deg` = board axis, `tolerance_deg` 15, target [0.7, null], on the detail tile.
8. **Checks follow the grain and stay inside a board.** *Why:* they form parallel to the grain [4]. *Enforce:*
   anisotropic cells clipped by the board mask. *Check:* `orientation` region = check, `tolerance_deg` 12, target
   [0.85, null]; `coverage` of check within gap_core = 0.
9. **Fastener rows sit on the joists or studs, two per board.** *Why:* code fastening [22][23][27]. *Enforce:*
   positions come from the layout (joist line, edge offset). *Check:* `spacing` region = fastener, axis along the
   boards = joist spacing +/- 5 mm; `components` `count_per_m2` = 2 x boards per m x joists per m, +/- 10%. Not
   applicable to hidden-fastener decks.
10. **Grey needs rain; sheltered and indoor wood does not grey.** *Why:* leaching plus fungi [12][14]. Sheltered
    faces stay yellow-brown in dry climates and go dark and blotchy from mildew in humid ones [4]. *Enforce:* the
    grey blend is masked by an exposure mask (layout covers plus an eave gradient); sheltered pixels follow a brown
    curve (dry preset) or a mildew curve (humid preset); interior variants use the ambering curve only. *Check:*
    `value_range` basecolor srgb255, channels r and b separately, regions exposed and sheltered (section 9).
11. **Damage reveals the next layer down in the same board, by depth.** *Why:* the skin is S1 (under 0.2 mm) over
    S2 (to 0.5-2.5 mm) [13][15]. *Enforce:* each removal mask carries its depth; colour = S2 colour (derived from the
    board's own colour) for depth inside S2, the board's fresh colour below it, blended by time since damage.
    *Check:* `value_range` basecolor srgb255 on fresh (deep, recent damage), channel r and channel b each within
    +/- 20 of the species' fresh value (section 7), `min_px` 200. Every species row has R - B above 40, so no
    neutral grey can pass both, whether the species is lighter (pine) or darker (cedar, redwood, ipe) than the grey.
    Species-free alternative (matcheck now has CIELAB channels): `value_order` basecolor `channel` chroma, regions
    [exposed grey face, fresh], ascending, `min_gap` 10 (est.: fresh wood C* is about 25-40 for the section 7
    species, weathered grey sits near neutral).
12. **Deck cup has one sign per moisture state, with per-board magnitude.** *Why:* the through-thickness moisture
    gradient is common to all boards [39]; ring orientation modulates magnitude [17]. *Enforce:* cup = state_sign x
    gradient_cup x (1 + ring_term), ring_term +/- 0.3-0.5 by pith side (est.), applied in both renders. *Check:*
    `height_diff` a = edge_band, b = centre, `local_mm` 150, signed for the state: median over blocks for the
    magnitude, and p10 over blocks at 0 or more for the dry-state sign (section 9).
13. **Finish loss reveals the substrate of the same board; penetrating finish has no step; penetrating finish is
    more saturated than weathered grey.** The chroma order is certain (oil intensifies chroma like water [16]; grey is
    near-neutral, section 4.1), so it is a hard check (`finish_chroma_order`, section 9) even though the C* values are
    estimates. The L* order is not certain across species: oiled pale wood can be lighter than a mildewed grey face, so
    hard-check L* only when the spec's own species values fix it. Where boards are refinished one at a time, also check
    the rendered colour per board against the finish mask (`finish_per_board`); a check on the finish mask alone only
    proves the mask is per board. *Why:* a film is a
    deposit [30]; penetrating finishes form no film [4][38]. *Enforce:* the film mask is a deposit; penetrating
    finish changes albedo and roughness only; exposed wood comes from the board's own layers. *Check:* `order` upper
    = film, lower = film_ring (bare wood next to it), `lower_stat` p50, `neighborhood_mm` 10, `margin_mm` 0.05
    (`min_px` 200); `envelope` covers the penetrating case.
14. **Random lengths with real stagger (floors).** *Why:* NWFA racking rules [25][26]. *Enforce:* the generator
    asserts stagger of at least max(152 mm, 2 x board width), no equal offsets in successive rows and a minimum
    length of 230 mm [7]. *Check:* `run_length` region = board, axis along the boards, `min_mm` 100, p05 of 230 mm
    or more.

## 6. Common procedural mistakes
| Wrong (what procedural materials often do) | Right (physics) | How to fix it in the graph |
|---|---|---|
| Weathering widens the dark gaps (blurred or eroded gap mask) | Gaps change only with moisture state; wear rounds the board arris inside the footprint [7][23] | Gap mask from the layout only; edge rounding from an inward distance field; `mask_invariance` |
| Latewood ridges or knots added as bumps on top | They stand proud because earlywood and the surrounding wood went down [1][3]; early raised grain is a small moisture-state recovery [40] | Subtract density-weighted erosion; raised grain in the state shared with `nowear` |
| Relief of 2-5 mm on a 10-year deck | 0.1-0.6 mm after 6-10 years; WRC and redwood stay near 0.6 mm; 1-2 mm only after decades on DF or SP (est.) [1][3] | Drive relief from age x rate tables per species (section 4.2) |
| Grey applied everywhere as desaturate-and-lighten | Grey needs rain plus fungi; sheltered wood stays brown (dry) or mildews (humid); interior wood ambers [4][12][14] | Exposure mask from layout covers and eaves; interior variants never grey |
| One grain texture tiled across the whole floor | Each board has its own rings, cut type, sapwood stripe and colour [5][10] | Per-board seed, board-local coordinates, mix of flat and quarter cuts |
| Cathedrals on every board, centred and symmetric | Cathedrals only on flat-sawn faces, offset and nested, with runout to straight lines near the edges [5] | 3D ring function with per-board pith offset (section 10) |
| Grain lines continue across neighbouring boards | Grain is discontinuous at every edge and end joint | New seed per board ID |
| Random crack noise | Checks run along the grain, mostly on flat-grain faces, inside one board [4][12] | Anisotropic cells stretched along the grain, masked by the board |
| Grey under chips, or every scuff shows pale fresh wood | Shallow wear shows brown S2; only damage deeper than 0.5-2.5 mm shows fresh wood [15] | Colour by removal depth: S1, S2, then the board's fresh colour |
| Fresh damage painted lighter than the grey on every species | Fresh wood differs in hue and saturation; cedar, redwood and ipe can be darker than grey (section 7) | Fresh colour from the species row; check per channel |
| Fasteners scattered at random, or none at all | Two per board on every joist, in straight rows [22][23]; none on the face with hidden clips | Fastener positions from joist lines and edge offsets |
| Visible nail heads all over a hardwood floor | Blind-nailed through the tongue; face nails only along the walls [25] | No fastener layer on floor variants except near the walls and on historic face-nailed planks |
| Floor end joints aligned (H-joints) or regular stair-steps | Random lengths, stagger of at least 2 x width, no equal offsets [25] | Constrained random lengths in the layout generator |
| Gap rendered as flat black | The joist top shows at the joist lines, along with debris and moss [32] | Gap floor material = joist strip + debris + AO |
| Moss and mildew sprinkled on sunny tops | They grow in shade, in gaps, at board ends and in cupped low points [4] | Bio mask = shade x (gap, low point, end) x moisture |
| Paint peeling in round blotches | Paint peels in strips along the grain, from latewood, ends and lower edges, and the south/west walls first [4][30] | Peel mask = latewood x flat-grain x exposure, stretched along the grain |
| Oiled deck modelled as a glossy raised film that peels at edges | Oils, WRPs and semitransparent stains soak in and form no film [4][38] | Penetrating finish changes albedo and roughness only, by per-board finish state |
| Uniform finish dulling | Wear follows traffic lanes, doorways and nosings (est.) | Traffic mask (paths) drives roughness and finish loss |
| Random cup sign board to board, or identical cup on every board | Dry decks cup edges-up board after board (gradient), magnitude varying by ring orientation [17][39] | Signed state cup x (1 + per-board ring term) |
| Wet = only darker | Wet is darker and more saturated, with puddles in cups and end grain wet longest [4][16] | Wet preset: Delta L*, Delta b*, low roughness in puddles |
| Iron stain on every fastener of every species | Needs steel fasteners plus tannin; none with stainless [4][29] | Toggle by fastener metal and species |

## 8. Variants and presets
**Exterior ladder (decking, cladding):**
- **new** (0-3 months): colour stage 0 (4.1); planed knife marks or rough-sawn marks; raised grain after the first
  wettings (0.05-0.3 mm, est.); manufactured edges; knots flush or slightly proud in the dry state; seasoning checks
  only within grade [21]; design gaps; bright fasteners; no deposits; finish intact if any.
- **weathered** (decks 3-10 years, siding 5-20 years): grey exposed faces and brown or mildewed sheltered ones
  (stage 1-2); relief 0.1-0.6 mm; knots 0.05-0.8 mm proud; fine checks on flat-grain faces; dry-state cup 0.2-1.5 mm
  edges-up (est.); seasonal gaps; iron halos (steel fasteners only); mildew in shade; debris in gaps; penetrating
  finish faded and worn on paths; paint chalked and cracked along the latewood, peeling at ends and lower edges.
- **distressed** (decades; barn siding 50-100 years): relief 0.4-0.8 mm in WRC and redwood, 0.5-2 mm in DF and SP
  (est. above 0.9); knots 0.3-2.5 mm proud; covered strips 3-10 mm proud; wide checks, end splits, shelling; cup
  0.8-3 mm (est.); nails 1-5 mm proud, rust streaks, missing knots (holes); decay at ends and joints; moss and lichen;
  paint largely gone.
- **maintained / mixed** (any age): per-board finish state from the layout (bare grey, freshly oiled, oil worn,
  replaced new), cleaned or power-washed areas, sanded patches (4.13). Check colour per state class, not across the
  mix.

**Interior floor ladder:**
- **new**: sanded and finished at the chosen sheen.
- **lived-in**: dull lanes, micro-scratches, dents in softwood, slight ambering, seasonal gaps of 0-1.6 mm.
- **worn or antique**: finish worn through in lanes and doorways; dirty grey-brown bare wood; softwood hollows of
  1-5 mm with latewood ridges and knots proud; dirt and wax in gaps; face nails on historic planks; on repeatedly
  sanded T&G, broken groove lips showing tongues (est.).

**Interview questions (recommended default in brackets):**
1. Product: deck, boardwalk, interior floor, lap siding, board-and-batten or barn siding? [deck]
2. Species? [pressure-treated southern pine for decks, red oak for floors, WRC for siding]
3. Cut mix: share of flat-grain faces? [70% flat / 30% rift and quarter (est.); quartersawn oak: 100% with fleck]
4. Grade or knottiness: clear, select or knotty (2 Common)? [2 Common for decks and siding, Select for floors]
5. Finish class: none, penetrating (oil, water repellent, semitransparent stain) or film (paint, solid stain,
   clear)? A penetrating finish darkens and saturates but adds no height. [none for decks; satin polyurethane
   (24 GU) for floors]
6. Maintenance history: uniform, or a per-board mix (replaced boards, refinished at different dates, areas under
   furniture), cleaned, power-washed or sanded? [uniform]
7. Age and climate: years in service; wet temperate (dark mildew grey) or arid/coastal (silvery)? [8 years, temperate]
8. Exposure: horizontal or vertical, sun or shade, eave overhang, height above ground? [full sun, horizontal]
9. Fasteners: face screws (stainless, galvanized, plain steel) or hidden clips? [galvanized screws; stainless on cedar]
10. Board width, gap and joist spacing? [145 mm boards, 5 mm gap, 400 mm joists]
11. Moisture state: dry (cupped edges-up, gaps open) or after rain (flat or crowned, darker)? [dry]
12. Viewing distance: hero close-up or mid-distance? [mid: 2.4 m tile; close-up: 1.2 m tile]
13. Normal format? [DirectX]

## 10. Substance Designer build notes
- **Layout generator.** Boards run along U. Decks: board lengths are whole joist spans (3 or more [23]), joints sit
  only on joist lines, and adjacent rows never share a joint line [24]. Floors: random lengths from 230 to 2,600 mm
  [7] with stagger of at least max(152 mm, 2 x board width) and no equal offsets in successive rows [25][26].
  Siding: course butts at the exposure pitch. Draw exact rectangles with an FX-Map or a Pixel Processor that hashes
  the row index, then derive per-board data with Flood Fill: Flood Fill to Random Grayscale (seed), Flood Fill to
  Gradient (board-local U) and Flood Fill to BBox Size. The distance-to-edge field comes from the layout gap mask.
  Per-board finish state and board age are a random or a user preset per board ID (Flood Fill to Random Grayscale,
  quantised), exported as layout masks.
- **Grain from a 3D ring function** (Pixel Processor, board-local mm). Per-board values come from the seed:
  cut type (flat / rift / quarter in the section 8 mix); pith offset across the board y_p and depth z_p (flat-sawn:
  z_p 30-200 mm, y_p within +/- 30 mm of the centre; quartersawn: pith in-plane to one side; est.); pith side up or
  down (cup ring_term, shelling and raised-grain susceptibility); taper k 0.002-0.007 mm of radius per mm (est.,
  section 1.1); ring width w and latewood fraction 1/3-1/2 [20]; a low-frequency warp.

  Compute r = sqrt((v - y_p)^2 + (z - z_p)^2) - k*u + warp and t = r / w. Latewood = a step on frac(t): sharp for
  SYP, DF, larch and redwood, soft for WRC, spruce and fir [5]. Evaluate z at the **eroded** height, so erosion
  reveals the correct deeper slice (invariant 7). Cathedrals, runout and quartersawn stripes all fall out of this
  geometry. For oak, add rays as thin streaks along the grain, 10-50 mm long [33], strongest where the face is near
  radial.
- **Knots.** Diameter at or below the grade limit [21]. Positions cluster in whorls (same u across a board, est.).
  Round on flat faces, stretched 3-6:1 into spike knots on quarter faces (est.). Bend the rings around each knot by
  adding a radial bump to r over 2-3 knot radii (est.). Encased knots get a dark boundary ring [5]. Export mask_knot.
- **Height composition order** (16-bit, mm-calibrated):
  1. board top plus per-board thickness noise (+/- 0.3 mm, est.) plus the moisture state: signed cup, raised grain
     on latewood (pith-side flat-grain strongest), knot shrinkage relief;
  2. minus the edge profile from the distance field (RED radius 6.4 or 9.5 mm [7]);
  3. minus knife marks across the grain (1.2-2 mm pitch [34]; amplitude 5-20 um, est.) only at 0.5 mm/px or finer,
     or saw marks;
  4. min(h, h - erosion), where erosion = age x exposure x (rate_EW x (1 - LW) + rate_LW x LW) x (1 - knot) x
     (1 - cover);
  5. minus traffic wear, dents, power-wash furrows and sanding (a planar cut: h = min(h, sand_level));
  6. minus checks, decay and holes, all clipped to the board; clamp board pixels to gap floor + 0.5 mm except on the
     hole and decay masks;
  7. select the gap floor (joist strip at joist lines, debris, darkness) by the layout gap mask with a hard mask,
     never a blur;
  8. add deposits on their own masks: film finish, dirt and moss, fastener heads, shelling slivers.

  The `nowear` output skips steps 4-6 and the aging deposits but keeps step 1's moisture state and the gap state.
- **Layout-only masks:** gap, board_id, end joints, joist lines, fasteners, batten and overlap covers, edge distance,
  finish state, board age, and the exposure mask (covers x eave-gradient parameter). Nothing downstream may write
  to them.
- **Colour.** Per-board species colour, with a sapwood band at one board-local v. Latewood darker in softwoods (est.),
  knots darker. Removal depth d picks the layer: d < S1 depth keeps grey, d inside S2 takes the board's S2 colour
  (its own colour shifted toward yellow-brown, est.), deeper takes the fresh colour; blend toward grey by time since
  damage. Exposed pixels lerp toward grey by exposure x stage; sheltered pixels follow a brown (dry) or mildew
  (humid) curve and interior pixels an amber curve (4.1). Penetrating finish: lower L* and raise chroma by finish
  state, like the wet shift of [16]; no height.
- **Pitfalls.**
  - Warp or blur after the gap select widens gaps and makes fins. Warp board-local textures only, and use a normalised
    blur blur(D x m) / blur(m) for masked blurs.
  - A ring field in tile coordinates runs grain across boards.
  - Additive "weathering noise" breaks the envelope.
  - Seeds must wrap for boards that cross the tile seam.
  - Isotropic crack noise makes blobs; stretch it at least 10:1 along the grain (est.).
  - 0.3 mm of relief is only 2.5% of a 12 mm height range, so set normal intensity from real mm, not by eye.
  - A finish noise instead of a per-board finish state makes oiled and grey areas cross board edges.
